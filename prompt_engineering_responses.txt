I’m sorry to hear your laptop isn’t starting. Let’s work through a few quick checks to pinpoint the issue.

---

### 1. Basic Power Check
| Action | What to Do |
|--------|------------|
| **Plug‑in Power** | Connect the AC adapter directly to the laptop (bypass any power strip or extension cord). Ensure the adapter’s LED (if it has one) is lit. |
| **Battery** | If the laptop has a removable battery, power off, unplug, remove the battery, then hold the power button **15 seconds** (this drains residual charge). Re‑insert the battery, plug the AC adapter back in, and try turning it on. |
| **Power Indicator** | Look for any lights (charging LED, power button illumination) or sounds (fan whir, hard‑drive spin). Note any patterns (e.g., rapid blinking). |

---

### 2. Display vs. Power Issue
1. **Check for a faint display** – With the laptop powered on, shine a flashlight at the screen at an angle. If you see a faint image, the backlight may have failed.
2. **External monitor test** – Connect the laptop to an external monitor or TV via HDMI/DisplayPort. Switch to the external display (often `Fn` + `F8` or similar). If the external screen shows output, the internal display or its cable may be the problem.

---

### 3. Listen for Beep Codes (if any)
- **One short beep** – Typically a normal POST (Power‑On Self‑Test) – may indicate a display issue.
- **Multiple beeps** – Different patterns signal hardware problems (RAM, BIOS, etc.). Write down the exact pattern (e.g., “2 long, 1 short”) and let me know.

---

### 4. Perform a Hard Reset (for non‑removable‑battery models)
1. Shut down the laptop (if it appears to be on) by holding the power button **10‑15 seconds**.
2. Unplug the AC adapter.
3. Press and hold the power button for **30 seconds** (this releases any stored charge).
4. Plug the AC adapter back in (leave the battery out if removable) and try powering on.

---

### 5. Verify the AC Adapter & Power Port
- **Check the adapter’s cable** for fraying, kinks, or broken connectors.
- **Swap adapters** (if you have a compatible spare or can borrow one) to rule out a faulty charger.
- **Inspect the laptop’s power jack** for looseness or debris.

---

### 6. Gather More Details
To narrow things down, could you let me know:

1. **Make & model** of the laptop (e.g., Dell XPS 13 9310, HP Pavilion 15‑ek).
2. **When the issue started** – after a specific event (update, spill, drop, battery drain)?
3. **Any recent changes** – new hardware, software updates, BIOS updates, peripherals attached?
4. **What you see** when you press the power button – no lights/sounds, a single blink, fan spin, etc.
5. **Battery status** – does the battery indicator light charge when the adapter is plugged in?

---

### Next Steps
- Try the steps above and let me know the results (including any beep codes, LED patterns, or external‑monitor behavior).
- Based on your feedback, we can move to more advanced diagnostics (e.g., reseating RAM, checking the CMOS battery, or preparing for a possible service request).

I’m here to help, so feel free to share any observations or ask questions about any of the steps!
